## What cheap WordPress hosting actually costs

Two prices exist on every budget plan, and only one of them gets advertised. In 2026 the pattern across the market runs $1 to $5 per month at signup, then $7 to $18 per month once the promotional term ends. The entry price is marketing; the renewal is the product.

Some concrete examples, all from current published pricing:

- **Hostinger** lists Premium at $2.99/mo, but only on a 48-month prepay, which means $143.52 up front. Renewal: $10.99/mo, a +268% jump.
- **Bluehost** lists Starter at $3.99/mo on a 36-month term, renewing at $9.99 (+150%).
- **SiteGround** lists StartUp at $2.99 for the first year, then $17.99 — roughly 6x the intro rate.
- **IONOS** advertises $1/mo for year one, then $12/mo.
- **Fasthosts** is the extreme case: a 6-month intro term at $1.33/mo that renews at $9.26, a +599% jump. The shortest intro term produced the steepest climb.

Notice the trade hiding in these numbers: the lowest headline prices almost always demand the longest prepay. A $2.99 plan that requires 48 months up front is a $143.52 decision, and if the host turns out wrong for your site, your leverage is whatever the refund window allows. That's why the renewal figure and the refund window deserve more attention than the sticker.

## What are you actually paying for with "WordPress hosting"?

At the budget end, a "WordPress hosting" plan is nearly always shared hosting with WordPress preinstalled and a couple of extras bolted on. The label itself predicts very little. Three things actually separate good budget plans from bad ones:

1. **Server software.** Hosts running LiteSpeed get WordPress page caching built in via LSCache, no plugin needed. Apache-based hosts can match it with a caching plugin, but that's configuration you own.
2. **Backup cadence.** Entry plans range from weekly backups (Hostinger's entry tier, Bluehost) to daily ones (DreamHost, SiteGround). Bluehost charges for backups as an add-on on entry plans.
3. **Staging.** ChemiCloud includes it at $2.49/mo. SiteGround gates it behind GrowBig, a tier that renews around €27.99/mo. Same feature, wildly different pricing — which tells you how loosely the "WordPress plan" label maps to value.

RackNerd skips the relabeling step entirely. It doesn't sell a separate managed WordPress product; its shared plans are the WordPress plans. You install WordPress in two clicks with Softaculous, keep a standard cPanel, and inherit none of the "WordPress tax" — or the managed conveniences. Whether that's a good deal depends on which of those two sentences matters more to you.

## Five checks before you pay

Five minutes of checking prevents most budget-hosting regret:

1. **Confirm the renewal rate in writing.** The Fasthosts example above (+599%) is what happens when nobody looks. Whatever the host, the order page or plan details should state the year-two rate.
2. **Count the upfront commitment, not the monthly figure.** Hostinger's $2.99 requires $143.52 up front. RackNerd's Starter at $1.95/mo annual is $23.40 — a much smaller bet on an unproven host.
3. **Read the refund terms.** DreamHost's 97 days versus the 30-day norm is a real difference if you're unsure about a host. Never take a multi-year prepay without knowing this number.
4. **Plan backups from day one.** Entry plans range from weekly (Hostinger's entry tier, Bluehost, with Bluehost charging extra) to daily (DreamHost, SiteGround). Whatever your host promises, a free backup plugin plus an offsite copy costs nothing and covers you.
5. **Check the PHP version.** WordPress recommends PHP 8.1 or newer for speed and security. Any host still defaulting to PHP 7.x is a red flag.

## Cheap WordPress hosting FAQ

**Is $2-a-month hosting actually usable for WordPress?**
Yes, with realistic traffic expectations. These are shared servers, so your site lives alongside hundreds of others. That works fine for blogs, portfolios, brochure sites, and local business pages. It's the wrong category for high-order-volume WooCommerce stores or membership sites with logged-in traffic — those need resources a $2 plan can't guarantee.

**Do I need "managed WordPress" hosting at this price?**
Only if you'd actually use what managed adds at the budget level: better caching defaults, more frequent backups, staging, and WordPress-specific support. If you'd use staging weekly, pay for it where it's included (ChemiCloud at $2.49/mo or HostArmada from $2.49/mo with daily backups on the cheapest tier). Otherwise, judge plans on the feature list and skip the label.

**Is RackNerd reliable enough for WordPress if it's mainly known for VPS deals?**
The shared hosting is a standard cPanel stack — Softaculous, free SSL, 99.9% uptime target, 24/7 tickets — so the trade-off is fewer managed conveniences, not a sketchier server. The company's reputation was built on cheap annual VPS deals that renew at the same rate, which is the single most relevant trait for anyone burned by a 6x renewal elsewhere.

**Does cheap hosting include email?**
It varies. RackNerd's shared plans include email accounts on cPanel. DreamHost's entry plan charges extra for email, about $1.67/mo. If client email matters to you, confirm it's included before ordering, because a few dollars a month quietly erodes the "cheap" premise.
